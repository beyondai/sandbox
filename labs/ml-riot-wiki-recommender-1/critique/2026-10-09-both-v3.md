## Change or fix

### P1

**C1. Wrong decision metric.** (Modified by the user.)
- Evidence: AUC and accuracy on (1 positive, 4 random negatives) pairs
  (l.59-61, 73-77). "We're happy with the AUC and accuracy numbers"
  (l.79).
- Effect: random negatives are easy to reject. An "always 0" model gets
  0.80 accuracy, and the report has 0.85. A trivial method can also get a
  high AUC: a keyword-overlap or same-space rule separates a clicked
  article from a random one, but it does not find the best 5 of 42K. A
  metric that a simple rule can max is not a decision metric. The 0.88
  AUC against the 0.31 P@5 shows the gap.
- Alternative: recall@5, nDCG@5, hit@5, and MRR over the full catalog,
  plus coverage. Paired bootstrap over source articles. Keep AUC as a
  training diagnostic only.

**C2. The random 80/20 split leaks** (l.36).
- Effect: a pair clicked 3 times is in train with 96% probability, given
  that it is in test. Reverse pairs and future months also leak. All
  test numbers are too high.
- Alternative: a time split (train months 1-10, validation month 11,
  test month 12). Slices for new articles and unseen pairs. `[ref]` The
  reference uses 2 cutoffs. On the proxy, random thinning gave the click
  baseline nDCG 0.97-0.997 (`monkey-mode/report.md`).

### P2

**C3. The label is any A->B click** (l.26-27).
- Effect: most clicks probably follow a link already on A, so the model
  relearns the link graph. A click that bounces back in 3 s counts as a
  success.
- Alternative: tag each click by source. Separate or down-weight in-page
  link clicks. Require dwell on B (more than 30 s). Use a links-on-page
  baseline and filter.

**C4. Random negatives only, and pointwise BCE on a raw cosine**
(l.50-53, 60-61).
- Effect: the model learns to reject easy random articles, not to order
  close ones. Without a scale, the loss cannot saturate, so easy pairs
  take as much of the gradient as hard pairs. (See "Note: temperature".)
- Alternative: InfoNCE with in-batch negatives (1,023 free negatives per
  batch), a learned temperature, and logQ correction. Hard negatives
  later (impressed but not clicked). `[ref]` The reference uses hard
  negatives from its candidate rules.

**C5. 50 fixed epochs, no validation set, overfit** (l.68, 75).
- Effect: the first layer has 25.6M parameters, about 27 per training
  positive. Train AUC 0.97 against test AUC 0.88 shows memorization.
- Alternative: a validation month, early stopping, AdamW, dropout, 3
  seeds. Test a smaller input (SVD to 256 dimensions).

**C6. The complexity does not pass the gate, and the score is
symmetric** (l.38-69).
- Effect: a GPU job and a weekly retrain pipeline, with no measured gain.
  Shared towers with cosine give score(A, B) = score(B, A), so the model
  cannot learn direction (post-mortem -> runbook).
- Alternative: a cost table. Keep the network only if it beats cosine
  and co-click by more than the noise. `[ref]` The reference uses
  directional co-click features.
- Question to ask (was Q9): does direction matter? Expected: yes, partly.
  Yes: separate towers or directional features. No: shared towers stay.

**C7. A vector DB for 42K x 64 vectors** (l.86).
- Effect: a new service for 10.8 MB of vectors. Exact all-pairs cosine
  takes seconds on a CPU.
- Alternative: compute the top 50 per article in the batch job and write
  a KV table. `[ref]` The reference does this (about 40 MB) and keeps
  the model off the request path (`adr/0001`).

**C8. New and edited articles wait up to a week** (l.89-90).
- Alternative: embed each article on publish or edit with the current
  model (this uses D1). A promotion gate before each weekly model swap.

**C9. Weekly retraining on the panel's own clicks: a feedback loop, and
no impression log.**
- Alternative: log impressions, positions, click source, and model
  version. Exclude or reweight panel clicks. 1 exploration slot.

### P3

- **C10. The kNN returns article A and near-duplicates** (l.88). Exclude
  A, collapse duplicates (cosine above 0.9-0.95), use MMR. `[ref]` The
  reference rates this P2.
- **C11. TF-IDF fit scope and refit are not versioned** (l.43). Fit on
  train only for the test. Version the vectorizer with the model.
- **C12. BERT comes before the eval fix** (l.96). Fix the split, the
  metric, and the baselines first. Then a frozen encoder as a baseline.
- **C13. The feedback button gives a sparse, biased signal** (l.95). Use

- **C14. Article window (3 years) and click window (12 months) differ**

## Note: temperature (why it is important)

Added at the user's request. It explains C4 and Q12.

**What it is.** Temperature T divides the score before the sigmoid or
the softmax.

```
 no scale:     p = sigmoid(cos)          cos in [-1, 1] -> logit in [-1, 1]
 temperature:  p = sigmoid(cos / T)      T = 0.05       -> logit in [-20, 20]
```

- Meaning: T sets how sharp the output is. A small T turns a small cosine
  difference into a large probability difference.
- Other name: the "logit scale" s = 1/T. CLIP learns s, from 1/0.07.
- Serving: dividing by T keeps the order of the cosines. T changes only
  how the model trains. The top 5 at serving come from the same order.

**Why it fixes the problem.** Gradient size on the cosine, |dL/dcos|:

```
 T             easy neg   hard neg   learned pos   hard / easy
               cos -0.2   cos 0.6    cos 0.8
 1 (no scale)  0.45       0.65       0.31          1.4x
 0.2           1.35       4.76       0.09          3.5x
 0.05          0.36       20.0       0.00          56x
```

1. **The output can say "sure".** The range opens from [0.27, 0.73] to
   about [0, 1].
2. **Finished pairs stop pushing.** At T = 0.05, a positive with cos 0.8
   has gradient 0. With no scale, it stays at 0.31 for all 50 epochs.
   This adds to the overfit (C5).
3. **The signal goes to the hard pairs.** At T = 0.05, a hard negative
   gets 56x the gradient of an easy one. With no scale, 1.4x. The top-5
   job is to separate close candidates.

**In a softmax loss (InfoNCE).** T decides how the push is shared among
the negatives: weight proportional to exp(cos / T). For 5 negatives with
cos 0.6, 0.5, 0.1, 0.0, -0.2:

```
 T = 1     [0.29 0.26 0.17 0.16 0.13]   almost equal: like random negatives
 T = 0.2   [0.57 0.35 0.05 0.03 0.01]   focus on the close ones
 T = 0.05  [0.88 0.12 0.00 0.00 0.00]   almost only the hardest one
```

A small T works like free hard-negative mining inside each batch.

**The cost of a T that is too small.**
- False negatives get the most weight. The hardest "negative" is often a
  related article that nobody clicked. With 3,400 users, this is likely.
- Training becomes unstable: gradients scale with 1/T.
- A T that is too large brings back the no-scale problem.

**Standard practice.** T = 0.05-0.1 for normalized embeddings, or learn
T in log space with a clamp (for example s at most 100). Select T on the
validation recall@5, not on the training loss.

**For the interview, 1 line:** "Cosine is bounded, so BCE needs a
temperature. Without it, the model cannot be confident, finished pairs
never stop pushing, and easy negatives get as much signal as hard ones."
