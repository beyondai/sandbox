## Change or fix

### P1

**F1. No measurable goal. The metric chain is broken.** `[v1:M1, v2:C1]`
- Evidence: :12-13 ("Engineers say that they cannot find what they
  need"; new hires spend time searching). No number, target, or date.
- Effect: the team cannot decide to launch or to stop. It can optimize
  AUC and not help new hires.
- Alternative: one chain (D2).
  - Goal: engineers find docs faster.
  - Primary online metric: the successful-click rate of the panel (a
    click with dwell of 30 s or more).
  - Secondary metrics: searches after an article view, and new-hire
    time-to-find ("time to first useful doc" in week 1).
  - Offline proxy: recall@5 (F3).
- Context note: the goal can belong to the lead. Close this gap together
  with the author (see Context).

**F2. No baseline.** `[v1:C1, v2:C2]`
- Evidence: :71-77 show only the model.
- Effect: nobody can say if the 25.7M-parameter model adds value. The
  P@5 of 0.31 has no floor. The model cannot pass the complexity gate.
- Alternative: score 4 baselines on the same test set and metric. Keep
  the two-tower model only if it beats the best one by more than the
  noise. Ship the simplest option within the noise of the best
  (Principle 1).

How to build the baselines. All baselines use the same test set and the
same metric. Only the scoring function changes.

```
 click log --time split--> train months 1-10 | val 11 | test 12
                                 |                        |
            build scorer (no training, or counts only)    queries: each A with a click in month 12
                                 |                        relevant(A): the B's clicked from A (dwell >= 30 s)
                                 v                        |
   for each test A: score all 42K - {A} --> top 5 --> recall@5, nDCG@5, hit@5 --> mean + bootstrap CI
```

- **TF-IDF cosine.** Fit `TfidfVectorizer` (50K vocabulary) on the
  article text at the train cutoff. L2-normalize. Compute
  `scores = X @ X[A].T`. Remove A. Take the top 5. A sparse 42K x 50K
  matrix takes seconds. It tests: does training add value over its own
  input?
- **Co-click.** Count the (A, B) clicks in the train months. Normalize
  so that hub pages do not win: `cooc(A,B) / sqrt(n_A * n_B)`. Take the
  top 5. If A has fewer than 5, fill from TF-IDF. It tests behavior with
  no model. It is usually strong on head articles.
- **Links on page.** Parse the outgoing links of A at the cutoff. Rank
  them by their train click count. Take 5. It tests: does the label only
  learn the link graph? (F4)
- **Popularity.** The 5 most-viewed articles in the train months, the
  same for each A. It is the floor. A model below it is broken.
- **Blend (optional).** `score = cos_tfidf + w * log(1 + cooc)`. Tune
  `w` on the validation month. It is the cheap V0 candidate (F7).
- **Frozen sentence embedding (optional).** No fine-tuning. It is the
  cheap form of "explore BERT" (:96) and the V1 candidate (F7).

```python
# core of every baseline: score matrix -> top-k -> metric
def evaluate(score_fn, test_queries, relevant, k=5):
    recalls = []
    for a in test_queries:
        s = score_fn(a)            # shape (42K,)
        s[a] = -inf                # remove self
        top = argpartition(-s, k)[:k]
        recalls.append(len(set(top) & relevant[a]) / min(k, len(relevant[a])))
    return mean(recalls)
```

**F3. Wrong primary metric. P@5 is dismissed as "subjective".** `[v1:C4,
v2:C3]`
- Evidence: :75-81. "We're happy with the AUC and accuracy numbers.
  Precision@5 is lower, but recommendations are subjective."
- Calculation: with 1 positive to 4 negatives, a constant "no" gets
  4/5 = 0.80 accuracy. The reported 0.85 is only +0.05.
- Effect: neither number tells how good the 5 shown articles are. Pair
  AUC compares 1 positive with 4 easy random negatives. The product
  ranks 42K articles and shows 5. The only product metric is ignored.
- Alternative:
  - Primary: recall@5 and nDCG@5 per query article, over the full
    catalog, with a bootstrap CI over query articles. Ground truth: all
    B clicked from A in the test period.
  - Report the per-query ceiling and catalog coverage (the share of
    articles that ever reach a top 5).
  - Drop accuracy. Use AUC only as a training diagnostic.
  - Interview line: "you already have the right metric; make it the
    primary one."
- Why the AUC is high:
  - ROC-AUC does not change with the class ratio. It is P(score(pos) >
    score(random neg)). The 80% negatives inflate accuracy, not ROC-AUC.
  - PR-AUC does change with the ratio (random = base rate = 0.20). The
    report does not say which AUC (Q2).
  - The real causes: easy random negatives (another topic) and leakage
    from the random split (F5).

```
 test AUC asks:   true B  vs  1 random article  (easy: other topic)
 serving asks:    true B  vs  ALL 41,999        (hard: close neighbors)
```

**F4. The label learns the existing link graph. A click is not
"useful".** `[v1:C2, v2:C4]`
- Evidence: :26-27 ("a user reads article A, then clicks through to
  article B. That is a positive signal").
- Effect: if most clicks follow a link on A, the model recommends what
  the page already links to. Orphan pages, the hardest to find, get no
  positives. A click with a fast return counts as a success.
- Alternative:
  - Record the click source: on-page link, or other navigation (search
    then open, same-session co-read). Weight the other navigation up.
  - Add a dwell threshold (30 s or more), so that a positive means
    "read".
  - Exclude already-linked articles at serving, or report results with
    and without them.
  - Compare with the links-on-page baseline (F2).
- Condition: if link clicks are under about 30% of positives (Q3), this
  item drops to P2.

**F5. A random event split does not match production.** `[v1:C3a,
v2:C5]` (D4)
- Evidence: :36 ("Split: 80/20 random").
- Calculation: a pair with 2 events that is in test is also in train 89%
  of the time (0.32 / 0.36). With 5 events, about 100%. (B, A) also
  leaks into (A, B), because the towers share weights and cosine is
  symmetric.
- Effect: the test score measures memory. The 0.88 is too high. Future
  clicks train the model that is tested on past clicks, so the score
  does not show the weekly-retrain behavior (:89-90).
- Alternative: a time split: train on months 1-10, validate on month 11,
  test on month 12. Remove duplicate pairs before the split. Add slices
  for new pairs and for articles that are new in the test period.

**F6. No access-control filter.** `[v1:M2a, v2:C6]` (D4)
- Evidence: :85-88 return the 5 nearest neighbors with no filter. :9
  lists post-mortems and design specs, which often sit in restricted
  spaces.
- Effect: a recommended title can leak restricted information. Security
  review blocks the launch. A fixed top 5 per article does not work when
  each viewer can see different articles.
- Alternative: precompute the top 50. Filter by the viewer's ACL at
  request time. Keep 5. For V0, index only the articles that all
  employees can see.

### P2

**F7. The complexity fails the gate, and BERT is next.** `[v1:App BERT,
App overfit part, v2:C7]`
- Evidence: :43-48. The first layer has 50K x 512 = 25.6M weights (99.7%
  of the model), about 611 per article. Train AUC 0.97 vs test 0.88.
  :96 ("Explore BERT embeddings").
- Effect: overfitting, a GPU pipeline to maintain, and no proof of
  value. A heavier model on an invalid eval gives numbers that still
  mean nothing.
- Alternative: phase it. Each version keeps the same pipeline. Only the
  scorer changes. Each version must pass a gate before the next.

```
 V0  TF-IDF cosine + co-click blend  ---gate: beats popularity + links-on-page--->
 V1  pretrained text encoder, frozen ---gate: beats V0 by > noise on recall@5---->
 V2  fine-tuned on click pairs       ---gate: beats V1 by > noise, also on slices
     (shared pipeline: batch top-50 table, filters, ACL, logging, rollback)
```

V0: no learned model (days of work).
- Build the baselines from F2. Select the best (often the blend).
- Build the full product pipeline around it: weekly batch, top-50 table,
  filters (F12), ACL (F6), impression logging (N3), dogfood ramp (F19).
- Reason: most of the risk is in the pipeline, not the model. V0 ships
  value and starts the impression log.

V1: frozen pretrained encoder (the cheap form of "explore BERT").
- Use a small sentence-embedding model, for example `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`,
  `bge-small`, or `e5-small`. No training.
- Articles have about 800 words, more than the 256-512 token limit.
  Split each article into chunks of about 256 tokens. Embed each chunk.
  Average the chunk vectors (or embed the title plus the first chunk).
- 42K articles x about 4 chunks = about 170K chunks. That takes minutes
  on 1 GPU.
- Use cosine kNN. Optionally blend with co-click as in V0.
- Gain: semantics (synonyms, paraphrases) that TF-IDF misses, with no
  training pipeline.

V2: fine-tune on clicks (only if V1 leaves a gap).
- Start from the V1 encoder (or TF-IDF + 1 linear layer if V1 failed).
- Train on (A, B) pairs from the train months with in-batch softmax
  (F9). Take hard negatives from the V1 neighbors.
- Use early stopping on validation recall@5 (F10).
- Gate: recall@5 above V1 by more than the bootstrap CI. Also check the
  new-hire and zero-click slices: fine-tuning on clicks can hurt the
  tail.
- Then compare online with interleaving (N2).

The author's model is a V2-type model with no V0 or V1 to compare with.

**F8. The cosine score has no temperature.** `[v1:T1, v2:C8a]`
- Evidence: :50-53 ("cosine ... binary cross-entropy").
- Calculation: if p = sigmoid(cos), p stays in [0.269, 0.731]. The best
  loss on a positive is 0.313, and dL/ds stays at -0.269 or less. The
  gradient never reaches 0.
- Effect: training keeps pushing all positives to cos = 1 and all
  negatives to -1. In 64 dimensions, not all unrelated pairs can be at
  -1. This wastes capacity and slows convergence. It is P2, not P1: the
  order of the scores still works, and train AUC 0.97 shows that the
  model learned an order.
- Alternative: divide by a temperature, `s / τ` with τ ≈ 0.05 (or a
  learned τ). It is 1 line. Q11 confirms if a scale exists. If it does,
  the item closes.

**F9. Pointwise BCE with 4 uniform random negatives.** `[v1:C5, T2,
v2:C8b]`
- Evidence: :52-53, :60-61 ("4 random articles are sampled as
  negatives").
- Effect: BCE judges each pair alone. Serving asks if B is above all
  other candidates for A. Uniform negatives are too easy, so the model
  learns topic, not order. This explains part of the "high AUC, low P@5"
  pattern.
- Alternative: in-batch softmax with logQ correction and mixed negative
  sampling.

```
 L(A, B+) = -log  exp(s(A,B+)/τ) / Σ_j exp(s(A,B_j)/τ)

            B_1   B_2  ...  B_1024
   A_1    [ +     -          -   ]
   A_2    [ -     +          -   ]     cross-entropy, target = diagonal
```

```python
eA = F.normalize(tower(xA), dim=-1)          # (1024, 64)
eB = F.normalize(tower(xB), dim=-1)
logits = eA @ eB.T / tau                     # (1024, 1024)
logits = logits - log_q[b_ids][None, :]      # logQ correction
logits = logits.masked_fill(same_item_mask, -1e9)  # mask duplicates and A_i's other positives
loss = F.cross_entropy(logits, torch.arange(len(eA)))
```

- logQ correction: a popular B is a negative in many batches, so the
  loss punishes it too much. Subtract `log(q_B)`, where `q_B` is the
  share of B in the training pairs.
- False negatives: if B_j is also a true positive for A_i, mask it.
- Mixed negatives: add a small shared set of uniform negatives (for
  example 128 per batch). They cover the tail articles that in-batch
  never shows.
- Optional: 1-2 hard negatives per row. Average the A->B and B->A
  losses.

Negative sampling methods compared:

- **Uniform random (current).** Each of the 42K articles has
  probability 1/42K. Mostly tail articles, mostly another topic. Easy.
  Needs extra forward passes. 4 per positive.
- **In-batch.** The B's of the other rows in the batch. Sampled by click
  frequency, so mostly popular, plausible articles. Closer to serving.
  Free. 1023 per positive. Bias: punishes popular items, fixed by logQ.
  Zero-click articles are never in training.
- **Mixed.** In-batch plus a small uniform set. Keeps the hard, popular
  negatives and covers the tail.
- **Hard negatives.** TF-IDF ranks 10-100 for A with no click from A.
  Teaches close order. Risk: no click does not mean not relevant, so
  skip the top ranks and use 1-2 per positive.
- **Impressed, not clicked.** Shown in the panel and not clicked. The
  real negative. Needs the impression log (N3), so only after launch.

If the author keeps BCE, the pos:neg ratio is a hyperparameter (1:4 to
1:20). Tune it on validation recall@5, never on accuracy. Evaluation
never samples: it ranks the full catalog.

**F10. No validation set, and 50 fixed epochs.** `[v1:C3b, App overfit,
T4, v2:C9]` (D4)
- Evidence: :36 (train and test only), :68 ("Epochs: 50"). Train AUC
  0.97 vs test 0.88 (:75).
- Effect: each choice made on test numbers leaks into test. The
  train-test gap suggests training past the best point.
- Alternative: a validation month. Early stopping on validation
  recall@5 (patience 2-5). Weight decay or dropout on the first layer.

**F11. The vector database is not needed at this scale.** `[v1:App,
v2:C10]`
- Evidence: :86 ("Store them in a vector database").
- Calculation: 42K x 64 x 4 B = about 11 MB. Exact all-pairs kNN is
  about 1.1 x 10^11 multiply-adds: minutes on 1 CPU. Load: 3,400 users x
  about 30 views a day = about 3.5 QPS, about 10 QPS at peak.
- Effect: one more service to run, monitor, and secure, with no gain.
- Alternative: a weekly batch writes `article_id -> top-50 ids` to a
  key-value table. Serve with a lookup and the ACL filter. Use ANN only
  past about 1M articles.

**F12. No ranking stage, re-rank, or filter step.** `[v1:M2b, App
diversity, v2:C11]` (D4)
- Evidence: :87-88 return the raw 5 nearest neighbors.
- Effect: the top neighbor is the article itself (cosine 1.0). Template
  copies and near-duplicate versions fill the panel. Archived,
  deprecated, and already-linked pages take slots.
- Alternative: the standard 3 stages.

```
 A --> retrieval (embedding kNN, top 50-100)
         --> ranker: score each (A, candidate) with features
               --> rules: remove self, duplicates (cos > 0.95), deprecated, already linked, no ACL
                     --> diversity: at most 2 per space --> top 5
```

- R0, hand-weighted blend (V0 and V1): `w1*cos_text + w2*log(1+cooc) +
  w3*recency + w4*same_space - w5*is_linked`. Tune the weights on the
  validation month. Cheap and explainable.
- R1, learned ranker (later): LightGBM LambdaMART on (A, candidate)
  rows, grouped by A. Same features plus popularity, article type, and
  age. Label: click with dwell. Only after the impression log exists
  (negatives = shown, not clicked), and only if R1 beats R0 by more than
  the noise.
- Reason for the order: with no impression data, a learned ranker
  trains on the same biased click labels. R0 gets most of the gain.

**F13. Feedback loop: weekly retraining learns from the panel's own
clicks.** `[v2:C12]`
- Evidence: :89-90 ("retrain on the latest click data").
- Effect: the articles that the panel shows get more clicks, so they get
  shown more. An article that the panel never shows never gets panel
  clicks. The model learns its own past output, not what users need.

```
 week 1: model shows B for A --> users click B (because it is shown)
              ^                                  |
              |                                  v
 week 2: model learns "A -> B is strong" <-- retrain on clicks incl. panel clicks
```

- Alternative, simplest first:
  1. Log the source of each click (panel, page link, search).
  2. Train only on non-panel clicks.
  3. Later, use panel clicks with inverse-propensity weights.
  4. Exploration: for some views, 1 of the 5 slots shows a candidate
     from ranks 6-50.

**F14. No promotion gate or index versioning for the weekly retrain.**
`[v1:M4, v2:C13]`
- Evidence: :89-90 ("Every week, retrain ... and recompute all
  embeddings").
- Effect: a bad week of data or a pipeline bug ships automatically. A
  TF-IDF vocabulary refit changes the embedding space, and a partial
  index update mixes 2 incompatible spaces. The panels change for no
  reason each week.
- Alternative: promote only if recall@5 on the latest held-out week is
  at least the current model's minus the noise, and above the baseline.
  Version the model and its index together. Swap the index atomically.
  Keep the last model and table for a one-switch rollback. Log the model
  version with each impression.

**F15. Zero-click articles are only negatives.** `[v1:App windows,
v2:C14]`
- Evidence: :24 (articles from 3 years), :31 (clicks from 12 months).
- Effect: the catalog and the click window can differ. That is not the
  problem. With uniform negatives, an old article with no clicks in the
  window appears only as a negative. The model learns "old means not
  relevant", even when the content matches.
- Alternative:
  - Keep the 3-year catalog of live, non-deprecated articles.
  - Do not let zero-click articles appear only as negatives. In-batch
    negatives remove them from training. Mixed negatives (F9) keep the
    tail in view.
  - Report a zero-click slice. The content path (V1) serves them.
  - Ask why the window is 12 months (Q18). The learning curve (N10)
    decides if older logs help.
  - Use the article text as it was at the train cutoff.

## Missing

**N1 (P1). Requirements and cost analysis at the start.** `[v1:App]` (D5)
- Why: the write-up states no latency, QPS, availability, catalog
  growth, index build cost, or training cost. The design choices (vector
  DB, GPU, weekly retrain) have no requirement to check against. The
  analysis may show that the load is small (F11 estimates about 10 QPS
  at peak), but only the analysis can show it.
- Standard method: a requirements table before the design: users and
  QPS (mean and peak), latency budget for the panel, availability,
  catalog size and growth, freshness, and the cost of the training and
  the index build per run and per year.

**N2 (P2). Online evaluation plan.** `[v1:M3a, v2:M1]` (D4)
- Why: offline recall does not prove that users find docs faster.
  Offline click metrics often do not predict online value.
- Standard method: a user-level A/B test (panel vs no panel) for whole
  weeks. With 1,700 users per arm and p = 0.3, the MDE is
  2.8 x sqrt(2 x 0.21 / 1700) = about 4.4 pp. That is too coarse to
  compare 2 models, so compare models with team-draft interleaving
  against the TF-IDF baseline.

**N3 (P2). Impression and click-source logging.** `[v1:App feedback,
v2:M2]`
- Why: hard negatives, CTR, position-bias correction, and the F13 fix
  all need it. The feedback button (:95) gives a sparse signal only.
- Standard method: log the viewer, article, shown IDs, positions, model
  version, time, and the click source. Ship it with V0.

**N4 (P2). Slices and a segment check, then a first personalization
attempt.** `[v1:M5, v2:M3]` (D3)
- Why: a global metric hides the long tail and the new hires that the
  goal names (:13). With 353 clicks per user per year, heavy users
  dominate the log. The model input is only article A (:19), so every
  user sees the same 5 articles. Different users may need different
  articles for the same A (Q6).
- Standard method:
  - Report recall@5 by slice. Item side: zero-click articles, articles
    new in the test period, article type, head vs tail. Query side:
    users in their first 90 days vs others, and team vs team.
  - Segment check: for the same A, compare the clicked B between
    segments (new hires vs others, team vs team).
  - If the segments differ, this is the first personalization attempt:
    keep the item-to-item top 50, then re-rank with tenure, team, and
    home wiki space. Compare on the new-hire slice. It must pass the
    complexity gate. No user embeddings: 3,400 users and sparse clicks
    do not support them.

```
 A --> item-to-item top 50 (same for all) --> segment check
                                                 |-- same B's  --> no personalization
                                                 '-- differ    --> re-rank by tenure, team, space
                                                                     --> gate on new-hire slice
```

**N5 (P2). Profile of the click log.** `[v1:Q3, T3, v2:M4]`
- Why: these numbers decide F5, F15, and the P@5 ceiling.
- Standard method: count the distinct pairs, the repeat pairs, and the
  zero-click articles. Report the clicks per user and per article. Check
  for bots. Decide on deduplication and a cap on events per user.

**N6 (P3). Monitoring.** `[v1:App coverage, v2:M5]` Track the
empty-panel rate, catalog coverage, popularity concentration, exposure
by space, CTR, the share of panel clicks in training, and job health.
Give each an alert threshold and an owner.

**N7 (P3). Privacy of the logs.** `[v2:M6]` The log shows who read which
post-mortem or HR page. Aggregate to pair counts. Drop user IDs after
aggregation. Set a retention period.

**N8 (P3). Scope, stakeholders, and diagrams.** `[v2:M7]` (D3) Out of
scope: search. Personalization: only the N4 re-rank, after the segment
check. Stakeholders: the wiki platform team (panel UI, ACL API),

**N9 (P3). Reproducibility.** `[v2:M8]` Fixed seeds, a dated data

**N10 (P3). Learning curve.** `[v2:M9]` Train on 3, 6, and 9 months.

## Questions

Each question has the expected answer and what each answer changes.

**Q1. "Cannot find what they need" (:12): in which sense? Who is the main
user?** `[v1:Q5, v2:Q1]`
- Expected: new hires and engineers who do not know where to start.
  Success is less time to find a doc, not pair AUC.
- (a) No start page: a search problem. Improve search first. The panel
  helps only with the next step.
- (b) They land on stale pages: status filters and an owner per article.
- (c) New hires have no reading path: curated onboarding paths.
- Why: it sets the goal (F1) and the slices (N4).

**Q2. Which AUC (ROC or PR)? How is P@5 computed: which candidates and
which relevant set?** `[v1:Q1, v2:Q2]`, `[user]` for the AUC part
- Expected: ROC-AUC on the sampled pairs. P@5 is unclear.
- `[user]` Ask which AUC. ROC-AUC does not change with the class ratio.
  PR-AUC has a random floor of 0.20 here.
- On a pool of 1 positive and 4 negatives, P@5 is always 0.20.
- If each A has only 1 relevant B, P@5 is at most 0.20. So 0.31 needs at
  least 1.55 relevant items in each top 5 on average.
- AUC 0.88 gives a mean true-B rank of about 5,000. That is a mean. A
  bimodal rank distribution can still give P@5 = 0.31.
- What changes: over the full catalog, report the ceiling and recall@5.
  Over a sampled pool, recompute over all 42K.

P@5 needs a set of relevant articles for each A. That set is the
evaluation label, so this question opens the label discussion:

```
 P@5 = (relevant items in top 5) / 5
            |
            '--> what is "relevant" for A?   <-- the label question
                   |-- which signal?   (click, dwell, co-read, rating)
                   |-- how many per A? (1 click or all test-period clicks)
                   '-- graded?         (click count gives nDCG gain)
```

Follow-ups, in order:
1. **What counts as relevant for A?** One test click, all test-period
   clicks from A, or clicks above a dwell time? Expected: all A to B
   clicks in the test rows, no dwell filter.
2. **Is the label complete?** Users can click only what they see.
   Expected: no. So P@5 underestimates quality, and recall@5 is fairer.
3. **Is the training label the same as the evaluation label?** Expected:
   both are clicks. If the goal is "found a useful doc", they can differ.

**Q3. What share of clicks follow a link that is already on A?** `[v1:Q2,
v2:Q3]`

A user on A can reach B in 2 ways:

```
 (a) on-page link:  A --[link on A]--> B      the author already connected A and B
 (b) other path:    A --> search "..." --> B  the reader needed B, and A had no link
                    A --> (same session) --> B
```

- Expected: 70% or more are type (a).
- Why: if most positives are type (a), the model copies the link graph,
  the offline score looks good for the wrong reason, and orphan pages
  get no positives. Type (b) shows where the link graph fails. That is
  the gap the recommender must fill.
- High share: F4 is confirmed. Weight type (b) up. Exclude existing
  links, and add the links-on-page baseline.
- Low share (under about 30%): the label is closer to "related". Keep it
  and add dwell. F4 drops to P2.

**Q4. Does the model beat untrained TF-IDF cosine on the same test set?**
`[v2:Q4]`
- Expected: not measured. The gain is small.
- No gain: ship cosine + co-click. A gain above the noise: keep the
  model and simplify it (F7).

**Q5. How many distinct (A, B) pairs exist, and how many test pairs are
also in train?** `[v2:Q5]`
- Expected: most test pairs are also in train.
- High overlap: the test score is memory (F5). Low: still do the time
  split for production fit.

**Q6. "Recommendations are subjective": in which sense?** `[v1:Q6, Q1
follow-up 4, v2:Q6]`

```
 "recommendations are subjective"
        |
        |-- (a) raters disagree about relevance       --> label noise
        |       same user type, same A, different opinions
        |       fix: measure agreement, golden set
        |
        |-- (b) many good answers have no click        --> incomplete label
        |       fix: recall@k and a graded set
        |
        '-- (c) different users need different B's    --> personalization
                for the same A
                fix: N4 segment check, then the re-rank
```

- Expected: the author did not separate the meanings, and no golden set
  exists.
- (a) A golden set: for example 3 engineers rate the top 5 for 100-500
  query articles. Good agreement (kappa > 0.6): the low P@5 is a model
  problem. Poor agreement: offline P@5 has a ceiling, and the online
  metric matters more.
- (c) Example for A = "Deploy service X": a new hire needs the
  onboarding guide and the setup runbook; a senior engineer on team X
  needs the recent post-mortem; an engineer from another team needs the
  API doc. The goal names new hires (:13), so (c) touches the core goal.
- How to tell (a) from (c) with data: for the same A, compare the
  clicked B between segments. Different B: (c). Same B but raters
  disagree: (a).
- A subjective target is a reason to measure it, not to ignore the
  metric.

**Q7. Are there restricted spaces, and does the recommender run as the
viewer?** `[v2:Q7]`
- Expected: yes. Then F6 is a launch blocker.

**Q8. How many articles have zero clicks in the 12 months? How many

- Many zero-click articles: the content path carries most panels, and

**Q9. Who are the 3,400 users, and how many are new hires?** `[v2:Q9]`
- Expected: a few heavy users make most clicks.
- Few new hires: measure them with a cohort before/after comparison and
  a survey, not an A/B test.

**Q10. How are outdated articles marked?** `[v2:Q10]`
- Expected: not consistently.
- A status field exists: filter on it. None: down-weight by last-edit
  age and add a report button.

**Q11. How does cosine become a probability? Is there a temperature?**
`[v1:T1, v2:Q11]`
- Expected: no scale. Then F8 applies. A scale exists: F8 closes.

**Q12. How were the 50 epochs and lr = 0.001 chosen? Was any choice made

- Expected: defaults or the test set. The 0.97 vs 0.88 gap suggests

**Q13.** `[user]` **What pos:neg ratio do you use, which sampling method,
and why? Can a sampled negative be a real positive?** `[v1:T2, v2:Q17]`

**Q14. Training details.** `[v1:T3-T8]`
Sorted by how much each can change the result:
1. Do you deduplicate repeat A to B pairs? Do you cap events per user?
   Expected: no. Duplicates leak across the random split (F5, N5).
2. Did you use dropout, weight decay, or a smaller first layer?
   Expected: none. 25.6M first-layer weights for about 1M positives

3. Which text goes into TF-IDF, on which data is it fit, and is it refit

4. Do you retrain from scratch or warm-start? Are the lists stable week

   week over week.
5. Do you have train and validation curves? Which seeds? Expected: train
   loss only, one seed (F20).
6. Is a V100 needed? What does one weekly retrain cost? Expected: not
   measured. TF-IDF cosine needs no training and no GPU (N1, F7).

## Conclusion

Verdict: **needs rework.** Both sources gave the same verdict, and the
merge keeps it. The serving shape and the hybrid content and click design
are sound. But there is no goal and no requirements analysis. The label,
the split, the negatives, and the metric make the reported results
invalid, and there is no baseline. The model can be kept only after it
beats simple baselines on a correct eval.

Top changes (all P1, same order as the Summary):

1. **F1.** Set one measurable goal: the successful-click rate as the
   primary online metric, with a new-hire metric.
2. **N1.** Analyze the requirements and the cost at the start.
3. **F2.** Score untrained baselines on the same test set.
4. **F3.** Use full-catalog recall@5 and nDCG@5 per query article.
5. **F4.** Change the label: click source and a dwell threshold.
6. **F5.** Use a time split.
7. **F6.** Add an ACL filter at serving.
