# Review: Wiki Article Recommender report

## Summary

- Critique: `critique/2026-10-09-final-v2.md` (cited as C, M, Q, W IDs).
- Tags: `[user]` and `[ref]` are kept from the critique.
- Verdict: **needs rework**. The design shape is sound, but the results
  cannot support a decision yet.

Top 3:

1. **No measurable goal (C1).** Without a goal, the team cannot decide
   to launch or to stop.
2. **The eval measures the wrong thing (C2).** AUC and accuracy on 1
   positive vs 4 random negatives do not measure the top-5 job.
3. **No baseline (C4).** Nobody can say if a 25.7M-parameter model
   beats TF-IDF cosine.

The order: the goal decides the metric, and the metric decides if any
model, also a baseline, is good enough.

## Done well

**W1. Hybrid design: content input with a behavior label** (:26, :43,
:57) `[ref]`
- The text tower gives every article an embedding, also with no clicks.
  The click label teaches relations that word overlap misses.
- Evidence: TF-IDF input, click pairs as the label.
- Keep it: content only has no learned relations; clicks only have no
  cold start. Keep both in every version.

**W2. Serving shape: precompute and look up** (:85-90)
- A request only does a lookup, so there is no online inference and no
  train-serve skew.

**W3. A crisp problem statement.** 1 article in, 5 out, so "@5" metrics
are possible.

**W4. The training setup is recorded.** The run can be repeated and
costed (about 104 V100-hours a year).

## Change or fix (P1)

**C1. No measurable goal**
- The report states the pain but no number, so nobody can decide to
  launch or to stop.
- Evidence: :12-13, "Engineers say that they cannot find what they
  need." No target, no date.
- Alternative: goal: engineers find docs faster. Primary online metric:
  the successful-click rate (click with dwell of 30 s or more).
  Secondary: searches after a view, new-hire time to first useful doc.
  Offline proxy: recall@5.
- Why the click rate, not fewer searches: a panel click can be traced
  to the panel. Searches have many causes.

```
 goal: find docs faster (new hires first)
   --> online:  successful-click rate (dwell >= 30 s)
   --> guard:   searches after view, new-hire time-to-doc
   --> offline: recall@5 over 42K articles
```

**C2. Wrong decision metric**
- Question (Q2): how is P@5 computed: over all 42K articles, or over the
  5 sampled ones? `[user]` And which AUC?
- AUC and accuracy on 1 positive vs 4 random negatives do not measure
  the top-5 job. P@5 is the right kind of metric.
- Evidence: a constant "no" gets 4/5 = 0.80 accuracy. The report has
  0.85. A keyword rule also separates a clicked article from a random
  one.
- Alternative: recall@5 and nDCG@5 per query article over the full
  catalog, with a bootstrap CI. AUC only as a training diagnostic. P@5
  becomes the primary metric.
- Why the AUC is high: easy random negatives, and leakage from the
  random split. ROC-AUC does not change with the class ratio, so the
  80% negatives inflate accuracy, not ROC-AUC.
- Is 0.31 bad: there is no floor without a baseline. With 1 true item
  per query, P@5 is at most 0.20, so 0.31 needs at least 1.55 relevant
  items per top 5.
- "Recommendations are subjective" is a reason to measure (a golden
  set), not to ignore the metric (Q7).

```
 test AUC asks:   true B  vs  1 random article  (easy: other topic)
 serving asks:    true B  vs  ALL 41,999        (hard: close neighbors)
```

**C3. The label copies the link graph**
- If most clicks follow a link already on A, the model relearns the
  page's links, and orphan pages never get positives.
- Evidence: :26-27, "a user reads article A, then clicks through to
  article B."
- Alternative: record the click source and weight non-link paths up.
  Require dwell of 30 s or more. Compare with a links-on-page baseline.
- Click source: the referrer of the page view (link on A, search, or
  navigation).
- If under about 30% of clicks are link clicks, the label is closer to
  "related". Then keep it, add dwell, and this item drops to P2.

```
 (a) on-page link:  A --[link on A]--> B      already connected
 (b) other path:    A --> search --> B        the gap to fill
```

**C4. No baseline**
- Nobody can say if the 25.7M-parameter model adds value over untrained
  TF-IDF cosine.
- Evidence: :71-77 show only the model.
- Alternative: 4 baselines on the same test set and metric: TF-IDF
  cosine, co-click (normalized), links on page, popularity. Keep the
  model only if it beats the best by more than the noise.
- TF-IDF baseline: fit on the train period, L2-normalize, `X @ X[A].T`,
  drop A, take 5. A 42K x 50K sparse matrix takes seconds.
- If the model loses: ship the simplest scorer within the noise of the
  best (cosine + co-click), and build the pipeline around it.
- If it wins: keep it, write the cost table, and simplify it (C10).

```
 time split --> test queries A (month 12)
   for each scorer (popularity | links | co-click | TF-IDF | model):
     score all 42K - {A} --> top 5 --> recall@5, nDCG@5 --> mean + CI
```

**C5. The random split leaks**
- A random event split puts the same pairs in train and test, so the
  test score measures memory.
- Evidence: :36. A pair with 3 events that is in test is also in train
  96% of the time (1 - 0.2^2). Reverse pairs leak too, because the score
  is symmetric.
- Alternative: a time split: train months 1-10, validate month 11, test
  month 12. Dedup pairs. Slices for new pairs and new articles.
- Dedup alone is not enough: future clicks still train the model that
  is tested on the past. It does not match the weekly retrain.
- The validation month also gives early stopping for the 50 fixed
  epochs (C9, Q15).

```
 month: 1 ............ 10 | 11  | 12
        train             | val | test     (no future clicks in train)
```

**C6. No access-control filter, a launch blocker**
- Question (Q8): are some wiki spaces restricted?
- A recommended title can leak a restricted post-mortem, so security
  review will block the launch.
- Evidence: :9 lists post-mortems and design specs. :85-88 return the 5
  nearest neighbors with no filter.
- Alternative: precompute the top 50. Filter by the viewer's ACL and
  archived status at request time. Keep 5. V0: index only public pages.
- Not at index time: each viewer sees a different set, so a fixed top 5
  per article does not work.
- It is not a modeling problem, but it changes the serving shape.

```
 A --> top 50 (batch) --> ACL + status filter --> top 5
```

## Missing (P1)

**M1. Requirements and cost at the start** `[ref]`
- The report has no latency, load, or cost numbers, so the vector DB,
  the GPU, and the weekly retrain have nothing to check against.
- Estimate: 3,400 users x about 30 views a day = about 3.5 QPS, about 10
  at peak. 42K x 64 x 4 B = about 10.8 MB of vectors. 2 h x 52 weeks =
  104 V100-hours a year.
- Alternative: a requirements table first: QPS, p99 latency,
  availability, catalog growth, freshness, training and index cost.
- Serving from these numbers: a weekly batch writes
  `article_id -> top-50` to a KV table. Exact kNN takes under a minute
  on 1 CPU. ANN only past about 1M articles (C13).
- Why P1: a cheap design is known to be enough only after the estimate.

## Direction (P2)

**C10. V0, then V1, then V2, with a gate at each step**
- Ship the simplest version first, and earn each step of complexity.
- Evidence: the first layer has 25.6M weights (99.7% of the model).
  Train AUC 0.97 vs test 0.88. "Explore BERT" (:96) comes before the
  eval is fixed.
- Most of the risk is in the pipeline, not the model. V0 also starts
  the impression log.
- 800-word articles in V1: chunks of about 256 tokens, average the
  vectors. About 170K chunks, minutes on 1 GPU.

```
 V0  TF-IDF + co-click blend   --gate: beats popularity + links-->
 V1  frozen sentence encoder   --gate: beats V0 by > noise------->
 V2  fine-tuned on clicks      --gate: beats V1, also on slices
     shared: batch top-50 table, filters, ACL, logging, rollback
```

**C11 with C12. Loss and temperature**
- Pointwise BCE with 4 random negatives teaches topic, not order, and
  the raw cosine has no temperature.
- Evidence: :52-53, :60-61. sigmoid(cos) stays in [0.27, 0.73].
- Alternative: in-batch softmax (1,023 free negatives), logQ
  correction, a few uniform negatives for the tail, τ ≈ 0.05-0.1 tuned
  on validation recall@5.
- Cosine is bounded, so BCE needs a temperature. Without it, the model
  cannot be confident, finished pairs never stop pushing, and easy
  negatives get as much signal as hard ones.

```
 gradient size |dL/dcos| by temperature
 τ             easy neg   hard neg   learned pos   hard / easy
 1 (no scale)  0.45       0.65       0.31          1.4x
 0.05          0.36       20.0       0.00          56x
```

## Questions for the author

Q2 (with C2) and Q8 (with C6) are above.

1. **Q1. "Cannot find what they need": in which sense? Is the main user a
   new hire or every engineer?**
   - Why: it sets the goal, the primary metric, and the population (C1).
   - Expected: onboarding. Then the new-hire slice is the gate.
2. **Q3. What share of clicks follow a link already on A?**
   - Why: it decides if the label problem (C3) is P1.
   - Expected: 60-90%. Then change the label and add the links baseline.
3. **Q5. What did the stub filter (< 50 words) remove?**
   - Why: if it removed index and hub pages, the most-clicked targets
     and their clicks left the data (C7).
   - Expected: hubs. Then keep them, embedded from the title plus links.
4. **Q7. "Recommendations are subjective": in which sense?**
   - Why: each meaning has a different fix.

```
 "subjective"
   |-- raters disagree         --> golden set, measure agreement
   |-- many good, few clicked  --> recall@k, graded set
   '-- users need different B  --> segment check, then re-rank
```

## Other changes

1. **C14.** Article A is its own top neighbor, so 1 of the 5 slots is
   always wasted. Add a rules stage: self, duplicates, deprecated, ACL.
2. **C7.** Stubs are also removed as queries, and hubs can be lost.
3. **C13.** A vector DB for 10.8 MB: use a top-50 KV table.
4. **C16.** Weekly retraining learns the panel's own clicks. Log the
   click source and train on non-panel clicks.
5. **C17.** No promotion gate or index versioning. Gate on the latest
   held-out week, swap atomically, keep a rollback.
6. **C18.** No ramp, fallback, or kill switch. Dogfood one team, 10%,
   100%. Fall back to TF-IDF cosine.
7. **C15.** New articles wait up to 7 days. Embed on publish (W1).
8. **C8.** Zero-click articles appear only as negatives. Use in-batch
   plus a few uniform negatives.
9. **C9.** No validation set, 50 fixed epochs. Early stopping on the
   validation month.
10. **M2.** Online plan: an A/B with 1,700 users per arm has an MDE of
    about 4.4 pp, so compare models with interleaving.
11. **M3.** Impression and click-source logging ships with V0.
12. **M4.** Slices, a segment check, then a re-rank by tenure and team.
13. **M5.** Data profile: distinct pairs, repeats, zero-click share,
    bots.
